# Swarmfall

A roguelite swarm shooter played mostly on phones. The whole game is `index.html`: HTML, CSS and one inline script using three.js r187. three.js and its post-processing add-ons are bundled into `vendor/three.min.js` (a classic script that defines the global `THREE`), so nothing loads from a CDN. The game itself has no build step; only that bundle is built, and only when three.js or the list of names taken from it changes.

- **Live:** https://thescootai.github.io/swarmfall/ (GitHub Pages serves `main` as is; a push is a deploy).
- **Game artifact:** https://claude.ai/artifact/WHbiJLyLcGYMiu2Bddz8s4 (the same game, built with `node tools/artifact.js`).
- **Enemy guide artifact:** https://claude.ai/artifact/18YkQbSpHhSxGimJ2GmKNq (source in `artifacts/enemy-guide/`).
- **Upgrade guide artifact:** https://claude.ai/artifact/EALugShxKLeMhoE6pxT3TC (every booster and station module; written by `node tools/upgrade-guide.js` into `artifacts/upgrade-guide/`).
- **Repo:** `TheScootAI/swarmfall`. `main` is the live game, and ordinary changes go straight onto it.
- **Staging artifact:** https://claude.ai/artifact/TDjavC3eeknZRiZyJmQsWR. For a big change the owner should try before it goes live (the graphics upgrade was one), work on a branch, build with `node tools/artifact.js <out.html> "Swarmfall Staging"` and publish there. Merge into `main` only when the owner says so.

The `swarmfall-dev` skill (`.claude/skills/swarmfall-dev/`) has the step-by-step workflow for a change. Use it.

## What the owner wants from the game

These came from playtesting feedback; keep them unless the owner says otherwise.

- **Phone first.** Drag anywhere to steer and the ship fires on its own. Every screen must work at about 390px wide.
- **The phone's own gestures stay out of the game.** An accidental double tap brought up the phone's text magnifier (a glass bubble) over the playfield. The second tap of a double tap on the playfield now refuses its default (a non-passive `touchstart` listener next to the pointer handlers), which stops the magnifier and double-tap zoom; buttons, links, text boxes, the scrolling screens and the feedback panel always keep theirs. The first version refused every touch on the playfield; it was narrowed to the second tap because a phone only lets sound start from an ordinary tap. Steering uses pointer events, which this does not touch. Only a real phone can confirm either part.
- **Main buttons never scroll away, and a screen says when it has more.** Every screen ends in a bar holding its main buttons (`.dockbar`). When a screen is taller than the phone, the bar stays at the bottom while the rest scrolls under it, and a "More below" tab shows until the screen has been scrolled to its end (`fitScreens()`, `moreHint()`). The owner asked for this on every screen after twice missing things below the fold at the station. A new screen needs a `.dockbar` with a `.more` button in it, and its id in `screens`.
- **One hit ends a new ship's run.** Hull, shields and everything else are bought with scrap (station modules) or picked as boosters.
- **Two hull upgrades at the most** (`HULL_UPGRADES`), counting Reinforced Hull and Hull Plate boosters together, so a ship has 3 hull at best. **The station sells only one of them**: Reinforced Hull has a single level, at 90 scrap (three times its first price); the second, or both without the module, come from Hull Plate boosters, which stop being offered once the two are used. The owner's steps: "5 plus shield is nuts", then "only 2 hull upgrades not even 3", then, after buying a second station level on top of a booster and seeing the rule take it away, "for ship upgrades you can only buy one hull upgrade and then you could get a booster for two. And the cost for the ship upgrade should be 3x what it is now." A save that had bought a second or third level gets that scrap back on loading (80 and 180).
- **Colours carry meaning.** All enemy fire is the same red (`ENEMY_RED`), and nothing good is red. Anything good to grab sits in a pulsing halo: gold rings in a blue one are scrap, green crosses in a green one repair hull (the cross's halo was blue too until the owner asked for green, "so it is easier to spot from the scrap").
- **New enemies are introduced.** The first time a kind of enemy (or a boss) takes its place, the game slows quickly to a stop, the field dims but for a white spotlight ring on that enemy, and a card gives its name, what it does and what to do (`INTRO`, `INTRO_TEXT`). It stays until the player touches the screen afresh (lifts the finger and puts it down again: the owner's "press fire to continue"), then the game speeds quickly back up and that touch steers as usual. Two earlier versions were turned down: a "Got it" button, and a card that went by itself after a second and a half. Once per kind per save (`meta.seen`). **The first scrap ring, the first repair cross and the first missile are introduced the same way** (the owner asked; they were one-line tips before): keys `scrap`, `heart` and `missile` in `INTRO_TEXT`, found by `introFind`. The ring is white because a blue one would read as a pickup's halo.
- **Mites are the pawns.** They are the largest group in every wave and hold the front two rows. Stronger enemies sit behind them.
- **Mites escort the stronger enemies.** The owner: "sometimes have a couple of mites launch with the stronger enemy to be pawns in front of them. Especially things like the beetle and forming a wall in front of them." From wave 2, when a wasp, weaver, splitter or beetle leaves the formation it usually takes the nearest mites with it (`ESCORT`, `launchFromFormation`, `sendEscort`): two for most (60% of the time), three for a beetle (80%). The first version started on wave 4 at 30% for all but the beetle; the owner: "I was thinking more than escorting beetles. They could even escort wasps or others. Was expecting to see it even on level 2." They fly side by side just ahead of it, matching its speed (`e.lead`, in `updateDive`), so a beetle's three hold a wall in front of it while it stops to launch its missiles. If the leader is destroyed they carry on as ordinary divers. Not on boss waves.
- **The latcher.** The owner's enemy: fast, it "latches onto the side of your ship and will detonate after 2 secs". They asked whether it should be slammed off against the side of the screen (and what would be there, in space) or shaken loose. The fuse is now 1.5 seconds and a flick must cover 50 units: the owner found the first numbers (2 seconds, 30 units) "too easy to shake off. You need to shake a little wider as well as only have 1.5 seconds." Both ways off work: three wide flicks of the ship from side to side throw it off, and so does one hard run into the edge of the field, where the ship already stops dead, so nothing has to be drawn there (`LATCH`, `shakeStep`, `latchOn`, `updateLatch`, state `latch`). Shaking is measured from how far the ship really travelled, so it means the same at any frame rate and a ship pinned at the edge earns nothing. Thrown off, it is destroyed and counts as a kill; going off, it costs one hit and pays nothing. **It never hurts by touching, so it can be very fast**: after the first version the owner said "the latcher never came down. I think the latcher can be really fast moving since the only way it does damage is if you don't get it off. It is like the face hugger from alien." So it does not wait to be picked to dive: a few seconds after taking its place it leaps on its own (`LATCH.wait`), at more than twice a mite's speed. **It is crafty about its line** (the owner: "don't have it come straight down for the ship as it gets shot. Have it be crafty on the side and over"): it drops down one flank, about 100 units to the side of the ship and jinking, then levels off just above the ship's height and hooks in from the side. A ship that keeps away from it for 1.2 seconds has outrun it; one standing still is caught. **Its sounds are the cue that one is on you** (the owner asked for a good one): a rising chirp as it leaves the formation, a clack and thud as it clamps on, then two quick pips that come faster and higher as the fuse runs down; the missile alarm is a single pip, so the two are not confused. With a shield up it clamps on all the same and the shield takes the blast. If it cannot clamp on (the ship is blinking after a hit, or both sides are taken) it flies on by, harmlessly. The fuse runs on game time, so a pause or an introduction holds it. With the bench's "ship cannot be hit" on it still clamps on and goes off harmlessly, for practice. It is a dull steel mine with a red light: darker than the ship so it shows against it, and not gold, green or in a blue halo.
- **Fire from the formation: wasps first, the rest from wave 7.** Asked whether wasps should shoot while they sit, the owner settled on "we let wasps shoot from the formation but only wasps", and for a while nothing else ever did. Then, on wave 9: "only wasps are shooting still". So the rule is by wave: before `UNLOCK.formationFire` (7) only a sitting wasp fires, a dart now and then; from wave 7 any sitting enemy may fire, wasps still darts and the rest slow balls (the timer is `G.shootT`, quicker each wave). **In flight too**: the owner added "even flying enemies, the wasp only shoots", so from the same wave a diving weaver and a diving splitter each fire one slow ball (`balls` in `ET`); mites stay pawns and never fire in flight. If the owner meant only wasps for good, set `formationFire` beyond any wave a run reaches.
- **Quiet waves.** The owner: "one thing that Galaga did that was neat was to have certain levels where the enemies didn't fire. It is rare. Like every 14th wave maybe?" On every 14th wave that is not a boss wave (`QUIET_EVERY`, `quietWave`) nothing fires: sitting wasps hold their darts, a diving wasp has none, a beetle launches no missiles and goes straight into its diagonal dive, and lancers never charge. The swarm still dives and latchers still leap. The wave's opening words say so: "Quiet wave: they hold their fire". These are ordinary waves with no shooting, not Galaga's fly-through bonus rounds, which the owner tells apart: "they also had waves where there was no shooting. Like a mini bonus stage."
- **The training simulation: a bonus stage.** (Tried on the `bonus-sim` branch and merged at the owner's word.) The owner wanted bonus waves like Galaga's ("those were really well done"), kept apart from quiet waves, and shaped it: "Bonus should just be your own base ship? Maybe it happens while you are docked? It is like a holographic simulation to improve your aim?" So once the ship has docked after a boss, and before the shop opens, the station runs a drill (`SIM`, `startSim`, `simStep`, `simOver`, state `sim`). Forty holograms fly five lines of eight; each boss has its own stage of five lines. Nothing can hurt the ship: holograms do not fire and their touch does nothing. Only the bare ship's own gun fires: `recalc()` works the stats out as if no booster and no module were fitted while `G.sim` is set (`mlv()` and `ulv()` themselves go on telling the truth, because the shop and the booster draw read them). A hologram destroyed is not a kill: it drops nothing and counts only toward the drill. What it pays goes into the run's scrap, which docking then banks (not on a test run). Tests switch it off with `?sim=off`.
  - **The lines are round and come in three formations.** The first paths had long straight runs and a sharp zig-zag, all flown in single file. The owner: "The ones we have right now need a lot of tweaking", pointing at Galaga's bonus stages (a video this sandbox could not play, then screenshots: columns two abreast, two files of four coming in low from both sides just above the ship, files rising on a slant). So the paths (`SIM.paths`) are now arcs, a loop, a flat pass just above the ship, a rising slant, a corner-to-corner cross and an easy S; and a line's eight fly as a `file` (one after another), a `pair` (four rows of two, side by side, `SIM.side` apart) or a `split` (two files of four that mirror each other and take turns to come in, a beat apart). The shapes are our own, in the spirit of those stages, not copies of them. `node tools/sim-bot.js draw <out.png>` draws every stage's lines.
  - **A perfect score must be possible on every stage, on every phone.** Holograms come 0.42 seconds apart (the bare ship fires every 0.36), and an aiming bot that takes them in the order they come clears all five stages every time (`node tools/sim-bot.js` runs each three times; a bot that always goes for the lowest loses the rising lines, because it keeps leaving one for the next to come in). The paths never change from run to run: they are what a player learns. Phones differ in how tall the field is, so the part of every line near the ship is placed the same on all of them (`simYAt`: the lowest point of any path is `SIM.clear` above the ship); only the run in from the top is longer on a taller phone. Before that the bot scored 36 of 40 on a tall phone. How far the lines sit from the ship decides how hard the stage is, because a shot takes longer to arrive: at `span` 740 and `clear` 70 the bot cleared the fifth stage only 2 times in 6, and at 620 and 40 (about where the lines sat before the ship moved down to its usual height) 6 times in 6. After changing either, run each stage several times, not once.
  - **It is its own place, and the screen goes wavy on the way in and out.** Shown the first version (the ship's projection flying above the docked ship, flat pale shapes), the owner asked "It looks like this?" and then said: "The background should change to like a neon grid when in the simulation. And the docking station should not be there. Somehow we need to transition into the hologram simulation. Maybe just the screen gets all wavey and turns into the simulation and then at the end wavey again back to the station?" So after docking the screen ripples more and more until nothing can be made out (the `simIn` scene; `WARP.k`, the `wave` uniform of the last pass); at its height the scene is swapped (`startSim`, `simScene`): the station, nebula, stars and far rocks go, a neon grid takes the nebula's place (`gridMat`: violet squares, a blue line every fourth, no halo because it fills the screen), and the ship, turned to light, stands at its usual flying height. Then the wave dies away. At the end the same happens in reverse (`simOut`, the swap back to the ship at the station's lower port, `simBack`), and only then does the shop open. Like every cutscene these cannot be skipped or paused. With the effects off, or for a player who asked for less motion, the wave is a plain fade through pale blue (`#simWipe`). Targets and the ship wear `holoMat` (light only, brightest at each face's edge, in thin climbing bands; they glow, since no more than sixteen targets are out at once), and a still frame lies over the screen (`#simFx`: fine lines, corner marks and a count of hits; no animation, so it costs nothing a frame). The targets' blue against the grid's violet was my choice and has not been judged by the owner.
  - **Its words say what it is, and stay up long enough to read.** The owner: "The message at the start is too quick to read. It is a training simulation. At the end, when it goes back to the station say 'Training Complete' or something like that." The first answer was the same words held up twice as long; the owner then asked for more: "Have a press fire to continue for the opening training message. You are coming off a boss fight and need to catch your breath." So it opens on a card (`#simHello`: "Catch your breath / Training simulation / Nothing here can hurt you. / Only your ship's own gun fires. Hit every hologram for bonus scrap.") and nothing starts until a fresh touch or a key (`SIMS.wait`, `simBegin`, `SIM.hello`); the ship holds its fire and the game cannot be paused while it is up. The first hologram comes 1.2 seconds after that touch (`SIM.lead`). On the first build with the card the owner reported that it "did not have the press fire to continue message"; it could not be reproduced here (the card and its last line show in the test pictures). The likeliest cause was a thumb put back down as the ripple ended, which the card took as the touch after only a second, so it now takes no touch for its first two seconds (`SIM.hello`); the other possibility is that the page was an older copy.
  - **A hologram is seen before it can be shot.** The owner: "have the opening batch drop in faster because I shoot all of them before I even see them." A hologram coming in over the top edge now comes down two and a half times as fast until it is among the lines (`SIM.rush`, `e.rushIn`), and no shot can hit one that is less than 30 units inside the top edge (`SIM.show`). Back at the station the scene holds for a moment under "TRAINING COMPLETE" and the score, shown for twice as long as other words (`toast(big, small, slow)`), before the shop opens (`CUTS.simBack.hold`). The top bar reads TRAINING. From the test bench the station that follows is a test run's: nothing is banked or fitted, which the owner asked about ("it just goes back to the station and nothing happens. Is that just for testing?"). They then asked again, with a picture: "After the training I had 34 scrap but when I go to the upgrade screen I have 0." The screen showed the save's bank, which a test run never adds to. So a test run's station now also says what the run collected and that a real run would bank it.
  - **What it pays: a line, each hologram, and a perfect score, 30 at the most.** The owner: "If you get every target in a grouping you should get 5 scrap. Show the scrap for a second and then have it suck into the ship. Then at the end you get 1 scrap per target and a bonus 20 for a perfect score? Is that too much?" Then, on seeing it came to 85: "Maybe that is too much. You would get more for a bonus stage than all the grinding in the waves. Open to ideas. The mechanics are good. The amount of scrap needs tweaking." The mechanics: when the last hologram of a line is hit, a ring worth `SIM.line` appears where it went with its worth beside it (`simLine`, `#simPlus`), stays put for `SIM.lineHold` seconds, then flies to the ship from anywhere and is paid on arrival (`hold` and `home` on the pickup); at the end the card pays `SIM.per` a hologram and `SIM.perfect` for all forty. Offered three ways to set the amounts (small and flat; growing with each boss beaten; their own numbers with dearer shop prices), the owner chose the first: "Go with A." So a line pays 2, each hologram a quarter and a perfect score 10: 30 at the most. For scale, worked out from the tables with every enemy destroyed and every ring collected: waves 1 to 6 pay about 29 scrap, 7 to 12 about 58, 13 to 18 about 99, 19 to 24 about 126, 25 to 30 about 151. The owner would "rather up the prices in the store than lower the rewards"; no price has been changed. The tests read the amounts from `SIM`, so changing them needs no change to the tests.
  - **It ends on a result.** The owner: "I did not get any animation at the end of the round to show how many I killed and what my bonus scrap was" (it was one line of text that went by in under two seconds). Now a card counts up the holograms hit, then shows the whole lines (already paid), what the holograms pay, and the perfect bonus or how to earn it, with the scrap counter at the top going up as each line lands (`#simEnd`, `SIM.end`, `simTap`, `SIMS.owed`; the scrap itself is paid the moment the round ends). Like an introduction's card it has no button and stays until a fresh touch or a key; a touch while it is counting finishes the count and does not skip it; the game cannot be paused while it is up. The wavy scenes that follow, and a short wait for the finger to lift, keep that touch from pressing a button at the station (unconfirmed on a real phone).
- **Darts lean toward the ship** a little as they fly (`DART`), so they do not always come straight down. Each dart may turn about 6 degrees in all, so a sidestep still clears it.
- **A boss's scrap arrives in a line.** Each ring of the shower flies to the ship at its own speed (`sp` on the pickup), so they are collected one after another.
- **Clouds sit in the open lanes, and enemies have to fly through them.** A cloud is lobbed between the formation and the ship, never up among the parked enemies (the owner asked for this of the gas cloud; the flame cloud follows the same rule).
- **Confusion gives no new ability.** A confused enemy breaks off, chases the enemy furthest from it and rams it: the rammer is destroyed and its target loses half its health. The owner rejected an earlier version in which confused enemies shot at each other.
- **Flame Cloud weakens and never finishes an enemy off** (the owner: "just want it to weaken them slowly"). It takes a share of the enemy's own health, so it means the same on wave 3 and wave 30.
- **The sentry satellite is a booster only.** The owner first asked for two strengths, a simple one from a booster (Sentry Satellite) and a tougher, twin-gun one from a station module that levelled up (Sentry Platform), and both could be out at once. Later: "Remove the sentry satalite as a ship upgrade. Keep the booster one only". So the module is gone from `SHOP` and `UNLOCK_ORDER`, and the station sells no satellite. A save that had it gets back the scrap spent on its levels on loading (160, 460 or 940: `PLATFORM_PAID`) and, since it destroyed a boss to earn it, the next module in its place (that part was my choice). `sats` is still a list, of one. A satellite can be shot down and runs out after a set time.
- **Your own beams and shots are never red.** The Laser Drone's beam is thin and yellow.
- **The Laser Drone locks on.** The owner: "I was thinking the drone would target an enemy and shoot its laser at that enemy and stay locked on it until it died. It should be a smaller yellow beam and the enemy should catch on fire and do damage over time from the fire." It locks onto the nearest enemy in flight and stays on it until it is destroyed, out of reach or back in its place, then, after two seconds' rest (`LASER.relock`; the owner asked for "a little longer cooldown between shots" than the first 0.35, then for "another second"), takes the next (`LASER`, `updateLaser`, `P.las`, `inFlight`). **Only enemies in flight** (the owner, after trying it: "it can only target enemies in flight"): a diver (a leaping latcher too), a confused one, or anything a boss lets loose; and missiles, which it burns out of the air in a fraction of a second (the owner: "should target missiles"; `LASER.missile`); never a boss itself (the owner: "it should not target bosses"), never an enemy sitting in the formation or flying in to it, and never a latcher that has clamped onto the ship (the owner: "should not target latchers attached"): if it clamps on while the beam is on it, the drone lets go. The beam sets the enemy alight (`e.lit`) and the fire does the damage; unlike a Flame Cloud's, this fire does finish an enemy off. The first version fired a wide violet beam straight up its lane every few seconds.
- **One richer ring, not several, mid-wave.** An elite or a beetle, which pay more than 1 scrap, drop a single ring worth more, a little larger with a gem at its centre (`worth` on the pickup): the owner found the field too full of rings. **A boss's scrap is the exception**: a shower of single rings, as many as it pays. The owner: "it is fine to have lots of scrap. Those should be single pieces because they do not get in the way."
- **Released ships must not fly in straight lines.** Ships a boss releases spread to lanes on their first pass, then each comes back from a new place, at its own pace, weaving, and aiming a little off the ship (`looseFlight`). The owner: "they need a little randomness to their flights".
- **Sound inside the Claude app's artifact page.** The owner had sound in Safari and none in the artifacts, in the Claude app on an iPhone. The fix, confirmed by the owner: a copy of the game running inside another page (`embedded`, in the audio section) also plays the looping silent clip on an iPhone or iPad, because the audio session setting that keeps sound on in Safari exists inside the app's page and yet does nothing there. Keep both. The magnifier fix was suspected first and was not the cause. None of this can be heard from the test sandbox.
- **Sound must survive a pause.** The owner lost sound after pausing. Two guards: the sound switch is no longer beside Resume on the pause screen, and `audioInit()` restarts audio that a phone stopped or left silently stuck while the page was away (`audioStale`). This cannot be tested without a real phone.
- **Waves gather faster as they go.** Enemies fly into the formation 5% faster each wave (`ENTER`), levelling off at 2.5 times the first wave's pace.
- **More enemies in a wave, as reinforcements.** The owner asked for levels with more enemies, tried this on a branch and merged it. The formation itself cannot usefully grow past 8 by 5: a ninth column leaves it no room to sway, and a sixth row reaches the lanes the clouds are lobbed into on a short phone. So from wave 2, when the formation is down to 35% of its places a fresh flight fills up to half of them, under the words "INCOMING: Reinforcements" (one long word would not fit a narrow phone); from wave 10 there are two flights (`SWARM`, `queueFlight`, `reinforce`). Wave 1 and boss waves have none. A flight is built by the same code as the formation, so `UNLOCK`, mites in the front two rows and three elites a wave all still hold. A wave is not cleared while a flight is still to come (`G.flights`).
- **Three seconds between waves.** After a wave's last enemy goes, the next wave (or the shuttle) waits 3 seconds (`CLEAR.wave`; the owner asked for 2, then 3). `CLEAR.boss` is the wait after a boss, before docking: still 2.
- **Split Barrel grows in three steps.** The owner: "level 1 is an extra bullet every third shot. Then every other shot with three bullets, then every shot with three bullets for the levels." Then, after trying it: "Level 2 is still 2 bullets but every shot." So level 1 adds one shot to every third volley, level 2 adds one to every volley, and level 3 adds two to every volley (`SPLIT`, `S.split`, `P.volley`). The first version of these levels had level 2 as three shots on every other volley; before that, each level added a shot to every volley. The extra shots are added to what the ship already fires, so with the Twin Mount module (two barrels) the volleys are 3, 3 and 4 shots; the owner's numbers were said of a one-barrel ship, and whether four is wanted with the module has not been asked. Its card says what the level on offer does (`d` in `UP` may be a list, one line a level).
- **Burst Thrusters.** (Tried on the `burst-thrusters` branch and merged at the owner's word.) The owner: "Try on a branch a booster that gives you the ability to burst forward or backward some and then it will float back to its home position." A rare booster with one level (`dash` in `UP`, `BURST`, `burstGo`, `burstStep`, `P.dy`). A quick flick of the thumb up the screen lunges the ship 110 units forward; a flick down drops it 48 back, which is as far as it can go and stay on the screen. (The first reach was 60 and 40; the owner: "Burst should go further.") It holds a moment and then drifts home by itself, about two seconds in all, and the next burst is allowed once it is home: that is the only limit on it. On a keyboard: the up and down arrows, or W and S. The owner named no levels, no cooldown and no protection, so it has one level, no timer of its own, and it does not make the ship any harder to hit: `P.y` simply moves, and everything that reads it (the hit shape, the shots, the drones, the shield, pickups, the latcher) moves with it. What counts as a flick (`BURST.flick`): at least 34 px up or down within 160 ms and no more than 0.6 of that sideways, so that steering on a slant, a slow slide and the wide side-to-side shake that throws a latcher off never set one off; an unasked-for lunge would end a one-hit ship's run. Not during an introduction or a cutscene, and never in the training simulation (the bare ship). Unconfirmed on a real phone, and worth trying first: a flick up from the very bottom edge is the phone's own gesture for going home, and a flick down may drag the Claude app's sheet.
- **The Arc Caster.** (Tried on the `arc-caster` branch and merged at the owner's word.) The owner: "I want to add a booster or ship mod that is like an arc thrower from Helldivers. Not a lot of range but multi enemy kills." It is a booster (Prototype, three levels; `arc` in `UP`, `ARC`, `updateArc`, `arcNearest`, `P.arcT`), my choice of the two, and the name is ours. About every two seconds, if an enemy is within 220 units of the ship, lightning strikes the nearest and jumps to the nearest to that, and on: up to 3 enemies, each within 95 units of the last, for 2.5 times a shot's damage each, so a mite or a wasp goes in one. Levels add an enemy, 20 units of reach and half a shot's damage, and shorten the wait (5 enemies, 260 units, 3.5 times, every 1.6 seconds at level 3). With nothing in reach it stays charged, and sparks at the ship's nose so it can be seen to be fitted. The reach is short on purpose: the formation is never in it, so it works only on what comes down at the ship. **It must still be seen to work.** The first reach was 130, and the owner, having fitted it on the test bench, reported "nothing happened": played through the bench on wave 1, nothing came nearer than 189 units before the ship's own gun had it, so it never fired once. After any change to its reach, play a wave through the bench and count its bolts; the tests that set enemies down beside the ship cannot show this. It never strikes a latcher that has clamped on (that one has to be shaken off), nor an enemy still flying in from far off. The bolt is the Arc Coil's lightning, drawn twice over and held a little longer (`zapFx(pts, life)`), pale blue, never red. The numbers are mine, not the owner's: they merged it after the reach was lengthened without saying more about them.
- **The EMP Bomb (on trial on the `emp-bomb` branch).** The owner: "Make a booster that is an emp bomb. It goes out and detonates sending out a circular wave that disables ships hit by it to lose ability to shoot and steer so they just drift in the direction they were headed." A Prototype booster with three levels (`emp` in `UP`, `EMP`, `launchEmp`, `empAim`, `updateEmps`, `empHit`, `empEnd`, `e.emp`). Every 9 seconds the ship lobs a bomb; it bursts and a ring spreads 100 units from it; every enemy the ring reaches is knocked out for 3 seconds (120 units and 4 seconds, then 140 and 5, and a second sooner each level). **Knocked out** means: a diver or a confused enemy cannot steer or fire and keeps the heading and pace it had; one sitting in the formation is not picked to fire or to dive, a lancer's beam goes out and a latcher does not leap; a missile flies straight on. Its lights go out, it sparks, and it turns slowly, adrift, until it comes round. It still hurts if it drifts into the ship, except a latcher, which passes without clamping on. Bosses are not affected (my choice: the owner said "ships"), nor is a latcher already clamped on. **It is aimed, and that matters.** Lobbed blindly ahead like the clouds, it knocked out less than one enemy a bomb when played through the bench; aimed at the nearest diver, one or two. It now goes wherever its ring will catch the most, counting an enemy in flight as three sitting ones (`EMP.flying`), and catches about four a bomb on wave 1 and a dozen at level 3 on wave 9. As with the Arc Caster, the tests that set enemies down in its ring cannot show whether it does anything in play: after changing its numbers, play waves through the bench and count. The numbers are a first guess.
- **Volatile Payload never chains.** The owner found levels 2 and 3 "waaaay too powerful": one kill cleared the top half of the screen, because each blast destroyed the neighbours and each of those set off another. An enemy destroyed by a blast now sets off none, and the levels grow gently (`BLAST`). Later the owner asked that it "should only have a 25% chance of it happening": a destroyed enemy now explodes one time in four (`BLAST.chance`), where before it was every time. The `stress` group checks one kill in a full formation at each level.
- **More elites as the waves go, until every enemy is one.** The owner: "later levels need more enemies with the red shield. At some point all the enemies should have the shield." The share starts at 4% on wave 8 and adds 3% a wave, reaching every enemy on wave 40 (`ELITE`, `eliteShare`): triplets and lancers too, and an elite triplet's copies and an elite splitter's mites keep the ring. Because a whole wave can be elite, the ring is small enough to stay inside its place in the formation and does not glow (forty glowing red rings washed out every enemy's own colour). So that a wave of elites does not flood the field with rings or keep the hull topped up, an elite's ring (worth 3) drops only as often as the enemy underneath drops one, not every time as it used to, and elites give at most two repair crosses a wave. Boss waves have no elites.
- **Half the scrap from enemies.** The owner asked to cut the scrap dropped by 50%, except for bosses: `SCRAP_RATE` multiplies every enemy's chance of a ring; a boss's shower is untouched.
- **The beetle is a threat.** The owner asked for the ordinary beetle to be "a bit tougher" and to "shoot its missiles quicker": 85 health (13 hits on wave 10) and its two missiles 0.8 seconds apart (`missiles`, `missileGap` in `ET.beetle`). **It does not fly over its own missiles**: the owner saw it launch one and then move over it, and asked that "when the beetle moves it should go left or right and then diagonally". After the last missile it slides 70 units to one side (away from the ship when there is room) and then dives on a diagonal back toward the ship's side of the field (`aside`, `asideSpeed`, `slope` in `ET.beetle`). Its escort of mites follows it through both.
- **Pacing.** The booster shuttle comes after waves 1, 4 and 9, then after every 5th wave: 14, 19, 29 and so on (`SHUTTLE`, `shuttleAfter`; the owner's schedule, in place of every 3rd wave). A boss comes on every 6th wave (`BOSS_EVERY`). A boss wave always ends at the station, so the shuttle that would follow wave 24 does not come: leaving the station gives the rare booster instead. After each boss the ship docks and gets the next station module (`UNLOCK_ORDER`).
- **Five bosses take turns** (`BOSSES`): the Hive Core on wave 6, the Fracture Cube on 12, the Beam Warden on 18, the Siege Beetle on 24, the Triad on 30, then round again, tougher. Each is built on an enemy the player has already met (splitter, lancer, beetle, triplet). The laser boss comes third because the owner set lancers to arrive at wave 15, so players have met lasers first.
- **Cutscenes always play out.** A touch or a key never skips the launch, the shuttle, the docking scene or the wavy scenes into and out of the simulation, because a player steering by touch has a finger on the screen as each one starts. (The launch could be skipped at first; the owner asked for that to stop.)
- **A boss dies big.** Its death is a scene of about 2.5 seconds (`FINALE`): game time slows to a fifth, smaller blasts follow the first, and a long deep boom plays (`SFX.core`). The camera does not move: a push-in on the boss was tried, and the owner said the slow motion is enough. The ship cannot be hit during the scene, nor for a blink afterwards (`FINALE.grace`), because it steers at a fifth of its speed while time is slow. The boss's shots are not removed: they carry on afterwards.
- **Sound.** Firing is silent on purpose, since a constant shooting sound was annoying. New sounds should be short and soft. The one long sound is a boss's death, which the owner asked to be longer and deeper.
- **How the owner tunes the game.** They leave comments on the guide artifacts ("start at wave 15"). When you change the game, update the tables, the guides and the README together.

## Conventions

- **Never remove from a live array mid-frame.** Set `dead = true` on enemies, shots and missiles. `sweepDead()` removes them once per frame. Code that runs inside a loop, such as a hit clearing nearby shots or a boss taking its swarm with it, must only set the flag.
- **No allocation in per-frame code.** Use the `parts`/`spareParts` particle pool, the ring and lightning pools, the shared `SLOT` from `slotPos()`, and colour constants such as `ENEMY_RED_RGB` and `HIT_RGB`. Never write colour arrays inline in a hot path. Share materials through `M` or a small cache; do not call `pbr()` per spawn.
- **Use the existing helpers.**
  - `despawn(e)` removes an enemy without a kill; `killEnemy(e)` is a kill.
  - `diveToLane()` sends several divers out spread across the screen.
  - `repair(k)` restores hull; `clearEnemyFire()` and `clearShotsNear()` remove enemy fire.
  - `pick()`, `turnToward()` and `wrapAngle()` are small maths helpers.
- **Tuning belongs in the tables**, not as `n >= 12` thresholds scattered through the code.
- **Bosses.** A boss is any enemy type whose `ET` entry has `boss: true`; test `e.def.boss`, never the type name.
  - A fight may have several bodies (the Fracture Cube's pieces, the Triad's three cores, which share one `hub` record for position, spin and attack timers). `G.boss` is any one live body, `bossLeft()` adds up the health of the whole fight for the bar, and `killEnemy()` runs the death scene, the scrap shower and `G.bossDown` only when the last body goes.
  - `bossLv(e)` is 0 the first time a boss is met, 1 the second, and so on: scale attacks by it, not by `e.tier`, which is already 2 or 3 at a later boss's first meeting. Health scales by tier.
  - Besides its introduction, a boss has a one-line `tip` in `BOSSES`; it shows under the name each time the boss arrives.
  - A boss's sweep across the top is not the same every time. The owner: "the hive core and maybe other bosses always follow the same path. Add a little randomness to it." Every boss (and the Triad's hub, and the Fracture Cube while whole) gets its place from `bossSway()`: still a swing from side to side, but it sets off left or right by chance, and every couple of seconds takes a new pace, a new reach, and three times in ten turns back (`SWAY`). It starts from the middle, where every entrance ends, and at its fastest moves at well under half the ship's speed. A new boss that sweeps should use it too.
  - How hard: the owner found the Fracture Cube and Beam Warden "a touch too easy" on first meeting, asked for mites to come down while the Warden's beams are on, and for bosses to do more each time they are met. First-meeting numbers and their growth per visit are in `CUBE`, `WARDEN`, `SIEGE` and `TRIAD`.
  - Design rules the owner's preferences imply: one attack of the boss's own to read at a time (its helpers may fly meanwhile); every attack leaves a safe place reachable within its warning time at base steering speed (240 a second); gaps between beams must clear the ship and its shield (`BEAM_HALF + SHIELD_R` each side); bodies stay out of the ship's rows unless the lane is marked first (the Siege Beetle's charge: two thin red lines, and it stops following the ship before it drops); the model must look as wide as its hit circle (`e.rad`).
  - A glowing part cannot be hidden behind another part: the glow pass draws glowing things alone. Hide it with `visible` (the Siege Beetle's core while its shell is shut).
  - A new boss needs: an `ET` entry, a line in `BOSSES`, a kit in `BOSSKIT` and a model branch in `buildEnemy()` that hangs its moving parts on `userData` (an ordinary enemy needs only an entry in `FOE`), set-up in `spawnBoss()`, an `update…` function called from `updateBoss()`, a branch in the model-motion chain in `updateEnemies()`, checks in `smoke.js`, pictures in `shots.js`, a portrait, and a card in the enemy guide.
  - The Beam Warden's beams are `e.bm` and `e.beams`, not `e.laser`: that field marks a lancer everywhere.
- **Two clocks.** `frame()` passes game time (`dt`) to everything. It runs slow during a boss's death scene (`G.speed` is below 1). Anything that must keep real pace, such as shake fading or a scene's own timer, uses `realDt`. The camera is placed afresh every frame in `syncScene()` from `camBase`; never set its position anywhere else.
- **Graphics.** See the next section before touching anything that is drawn.
- **Debug handle.** `window.__swarmfall` exposes `G`, `P`, `S`, `meta`, `ET`, `UNLOCK`, `BOSSES`, `CLEAR`, `SWARM`, `ELITE`, `BLAST`, `SPLIT`, `SWAY`, `BURST`, `ARC`, `EMP`, `launchEmp`, `emps`, `burstGo`, `shipHit`, `bossSway`, `SHUTTLE`, `ESCORT`, `sendEscort`, `LATCH`, `SHK`, `SCRAP_RATE`, `eliteShare`, `shuttleAfter`, `wavesToShuttle`, `quietWave`, `SIM`, `SIMS`, `WARP`, `simPath`, `startSim`, `simBegin`, `ST`, `backdrop` (the nebula's sheet, which wears the grid in the simulation), `SIEGE`, `DART`, `CLOUD`, `SENTRY`, `LASER`, `SH`, `sats`, `clouds`, `launchCloud`, `GFX`, `FIN`, `camera`, `scene`, `recalc`, `nextWave`, `spawnEnemy`, `launchDive`, `fireMissile` and `spawnPick` for tests and the browser console. The game itself never uses it.
- **Writing for players.** Plain words, phone-sized lines. Every new enemy and boss gets an introduction: two short lines in `INTRO_TEXT` (what it does, then what to do about it), and for an ordinary enemy its name in `INTRO_NAME`. Scrap, the repair cross and missiles are introduced the same way (`scrap`, `heart` and `missile` in `INTRO_TEXT`). There are no one-line first-time tips any more.

## Graphics

How a frame is drawn (`draw()`, the only place that renders; the loop, the paused frame and the feedback screenshot all call it):

1. Everything on the `GLOW` layer is drawn alone into a half-size buffer and blurred by the bloom pass.
2. The whole scene is drawn into a high-range buffer.
3. One last pass (`MIX_SHADER`) adds the blurred halos, a slight colour fringe that pulses with screen shake and darkened corners, then tone-maps to the screen.

Rules that follow from it:

- **Glow is chosen, not automatic.** A mesh glows if its material was made with `glowMat()` or wrapped in `glowing()` and the mesh was built with `part()`. Shots, sparks and lightning are put on the layer where they are created. Big bright shapes must not glow: blurred, they flood the screen and wash out the colours that carry meaning. That is why the laser's white core (`addMat`) has no halo and shockwave rings are a thin shader band.
- **Colour rules still decide everything.** After any change here, run `node tools/shots.js <dir> "gfx=high"` and look: enemy fire red, halos blue, scrap gold, nothing good red. The backdrop stays in blues, violets and teals.
- **Quality levels (`GFX.q`).** 2 is every effect, 1 is the same at a lower resolution, 0 is plain drawing with no post-processing. The player's setting is `meta.gfx` (`auto`, `high` or `low`, on the pause screen). On `auto`, `gfxWatch()` steps down when frames stay slower than about 26 a second and remembers it in `meta.autoQ`. It never steps up by itself. A phone in low-power mode runs at a steady 30, which must not count as slow. `?gfx=high` or `?gfx=low` in the address pins the level.
- **Level 0 must always work.** If the device cannot draw into high-range buffers, or setting up the effects throws, the game falls back to it.
- **Lights are fixed.** `ambient`, `sun`, `shipLight` and `flashLight` are always in the scene, on both layers. Adding or removing a light recompiles every material and stalls a frame. For an explosion call `flash(x, y, power)`.
- **Materials.** `pbr(colour, glowColour, metalness, roughness)` for solid faceted surfaces; they mirror `scene.environment`. `basic()` for flat colour, `addMat()` for light added to what is behind it, `glowMat()` for the same with a halo.
- **Your own shaders** must end with `#include <tonemapping_fragment>` and `#include <colorspace_fragment>`, or they come out wrong at level 0.
- **Colours.** three.js converts colours given as numbers or `Color` objects from screen colour to its working colour. Numbers written straight into a buffer (particles, stars) are not converted: pass them through `lin()` first.
- **A three.js name that is not in the bundle** (`THREE.Something is not a constructor`): add it to `tools/three-entry.js`, then `cd tools && node build-three.js`, and commit the new `vendor/three.min.js`.
- **Instanced shots:** set `im.count` and upload with `commitInst()`; other buffers with `uploadFirst()`.
- **Models are kits.** (Tried on the `enemy-visuals` branch and merged at the owner's word.) The owner: "Take a stab in a new branch to upgrade the visuals of the enemies", then "And my ship and the docking station." Then, shown the first pass: "Do the bosses and the shuttle too." Each of the eight ordinary enemies, the five bosses, the ship, the station and the shuttle is a list of plain shapes (`FOE`, `BOSSKIT`, `hiveKit()`, `SHIP_KIT`, `ST_KIT`, `SH_KIT`): `[shape in geo, colour, width, height, depth, x, y, z, turn about z, about x, about y]`. `kit()` merges a list once into a single mesh with each part's colour on its corners, and `kitMesh()` gives it a material: `M.foe` for enemies, `M.craft` (shinier) for the ship and station, `M.foeGlow` for a second small mesh of lights. So a model of twenty parts costs no more to draw than one of two: every ordinary enemy is 3 draws (body, and lights drawn twice), which is the same as or fewer than before for all but the weaver and the splitter (2 before). Rules that come with it:
  - No negative sizes in a kit: a mirrored part is written out, or its faces turn inside out.
  - A light shows through whatever covers it, so a model with a light on its face must not turn its back. The mite rocks from side to side for that reason; it used to spin.
  - A part that moves by itself stays a mesh of its own: the lancer's eye (`userData.eye`, at `y = -13`, where its beam starts), the latcher's fuse (`userData.fuse`), the ship's flame and shield, the station's ring and its four blinking lights.
  - A model stays inside its hit radius (`def.r`), wings and legs included; an elite is the same model scaled up inside its ring. The ship keeps the outline `shipHit()` was drawn for, and the station's ports stay 55 above and below its middle, where the docking scenes put the ship.
  - Each enemy keeps its own colour (`def.col` is the colour it bursts into). No new part in enemy-fire red, and nothing ringed in blue, gold or green.
  - The hit-flash and the training simulation's hologram swap the material of every mesh in `e.meshes`, lights included.
  - A boss is the kit of the enemy it is built on, writ large and with more to it, so it still reads as that enemy grown up: the Fracture Cube is the splitter's banded crystals, the Beam Warden the lancer in a ring, the Siege Beetle the beetle, each Triad core a triplet. A boss's moving parts are kits of their own, hung on the same `userData` names the motion code reads (`core`, `ring`, `body`, `spin`, `shL`, `shR`, `eye`). The Hive Core's colour changes each time round, so its kit is made per colour (`hiveKit(tint)`, with `shade()` for its darker and paler parts). A boss now takes 3 to 6 draws where it took 5 to 7.
  - `part()` and the single-colour `M` materials remain for what is not a kit: pickups, the booster crate, drones, satellites, missiles, and the lights and flames that blink or stretch.

Not done, on purpose: the WebGPU renderer and node materials. They cannot be run in the test sandbox, and phone support is still uneven.

A new feature gets its checks in the group it belongs to, or a new group (add it to `GROUPS` and `ABOUT` in `smoke.js`, and to the table above). A group must not depend on another having run: start from `open()`, and set up whatever the checks need.

Every test page but the `intro` group's switches enemy introductions off with `?intro=off`, as `shots.js` and `portraits.js` do, and every page but the `sim` group's switches the simulation off with `?sim=off` (it would add half a minute to every docking), and all but the `gfx` group's use `?gfx=low`, because software rendering makes the effects very slow. Frame rate on a real phone cannot be measured here; say so when it matters.

Passing tests cannot see colour or layout. After a change to rendering or UI, save screenshots (pass a directory) and look at them.

## Publishing

1. Commit and push to `main`. GitHub Pages redeploys within a minute. **Go back and check that the deployment ended in success** (the `pages build and deployment` run for that commit), not only that it started: twice a run was reported as under way and had failed seconds later, and the owner found the live site two merges behind. That failure was a link named `tools/node_modules` committed from a second working copy (Pages cannot pack a link that points nowhere); never link that folder inside the repository, and the ignore rule now covers a link as well as a folder.
2. Rebuild and republish the game artifact: `node tools/artifact.js <dir>/swarmfall.html`, then publish that file to the game artifact URL above with `root` set to `<dir>` and `files` mapping `vendor/three.min.js` (the script writes it beside the page).
3. If enemies, their timings or their numbers changed, update `artifacts/enemy-guide/index.html` and republish it with its `img/` files to the guide URL. Re-render the pictures with `node tools/portraits.js` if a model changed.
4. If a booster or station module changed (`UP`, `SHOP`, `UNLOCK_ORDER`, `START_MODS`, the odds in `rollCards()`, or what one does in `recalc()` and elsewhere), update its note in `tools/upgrade-guide.js` if its numbers changed, run `node tools/upgrade-guide.js`, and republish `artifacts/upgrade-guide/index.html` to the upgrade guide URL.
